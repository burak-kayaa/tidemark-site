Your workday leaves a mark. Tidemark writes it down.

Tidemark runs on your computer and follows what you work on. At the end of the day it hands you the day's worklogs to check: which ticket, from when to when, what you did. Fix what is wrong, then send them to Jira or copy them into your spreadsheet.

Follow on GitHub For Linux first, then macOS and Windows.

Three steps, and the first is your normal day

You don't start a timer or remember to switch tasks. Tidemark reads the traces your work already leaves.

  1. Work as usual

    Your editor, terminal, browser and calls are recorded on your machine, and the day is split into sessions wherever you took a break.

  2. Check the suggestions

    Each session comes with the ticket or label it most likely belongs to, and how sure Tidemark is. Accept it, correct it or skip it. It learns from your choices.

  3. Log it

    One worklog per ticket and day, with a start, an end and a description. Publish it to Jira, or copy a table or a message for anywhere else.

Made for days that don't fit a timer

Real days switch between tickets, meetings and things nobody tracks.

Finds the ticket on its own

Keys like PAY-123 in branch names, window titles and URLs are picked up. Once a repository, site or server keeps going to the same work, Tidemark offers to remember it.

Work without a ticket

Not everything lives in Jira. Log time to a label instead: a client, a meeting, a weekly report.

A day you can correct

Add time by hand, change when a worklog starts and ends, round to the half or whole hour, and write the description yourself.

Any range, in any format

A summary of a day, a week or any two dates: as a table that pastes into a spreadsheet, as CSV, or as text for a message.

In your tray

A small icon shows how much is left to review and opens the app in its own window.

Descriptions, if you want them

Each worklog gets a one-line description. Claude can write better ones on request; you see exactly what would be sent before anything is.

Your activity stays on your computer

What you worked on all day is personal. Tidemark keeps it in one file on your disk and runs without an account.

  • Local onlyThe app runs on your machine and only answers on 127.0.0.1.
  • Nothing leaves without youJira gets the worklogs you publish. Claude gets a description request only when you ask, after a preview.
  • Private windows stay privateIncognito tabs are never recorded, and apps and sites you exclude are stored only as "private".
