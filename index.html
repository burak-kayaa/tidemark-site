Your workday leaves a mark. Tidemark writes it down.

Tidemark runs on your computer and follows what you work on. At the end of the day it hands you the day's worklogs to check: which ticket, from when to when, what you did. Fix what is wrong, then send them to Jira or write them into your spreadsheet.

Three steps, and the first is your normal day

You don't start a timer or remember to switch tasks. Tidemark reads the traces your work already leaves.

  1. Work as usual

    Your editor, terminal, browser, commits and meetings are recorded on your machine, and the day is split into sessions wherever you took a break.

  2. Check the suggestions

    Each session comes with the ticket or job it most likely belongs to. Accept the sure ones in one go, correct the rest, skip what isn't work. It learns from your choices.

  3. Report it

    One worklog per job and stretch of work, with a start, an end and a description. Publish to Jira or write it into the spreadsheet you report in.

Made for days that don't fit a timer

Real days switch between tickets, meetings and things nobody tracks, and get reported in a sheet somebody else designed.

It finds the work

Where a suggestion comes from.

  • Ticket keysKeys like PAY-123 in branches, window titles, URLs and commit messages, and the IDs of your own systems.
  • Your commitsA session's commits name the work and write its description, without any AI.
  • Your calendarA meeting away from the desk still counts, under its own title.
  • Rules it offersWhen a repo, site or server keeps going to the same job, or keeps being skipped, it offers to remember that.

It fits how you report

Jira, or the sheet your team already keeps.

  • Learns your sheetShow it the Excel file you fill in. It reads the columns, the choices in them and the date format.
  • Writes into itRow by row, each one shown first, into the file on your computer or the workbook in OneDrive. A row written before is marked.
  • Jobs without ticketsLog to a label or an ID from another system; add your own fields, like the customer.
  • Any rangeA day, a week or a month as a table, CSV, a message, or a timeline of your days.

It keeps the day honest

So nothing is forgotten, or made up.

  • RemindersAt the end of the day, and for earlier days still open. Back from a long break, it asks whether to log it.
  • Your working dayEach day's time against the hours you should log, with the days that fall short pointed out.
  • Correct anythingAdd time by hand, round a start or an end, rename a job everywhere, write the description yourself.
  • Ask your assistantClaude can tell you what you did this week or write your standup, from your reviewed work.

Your activity stays on your computer

What you worked on all day is personal. Tidemark keeps it in one file on your disk and runs without an account.

  • Local onlyThe app runs on your machine and only answers on 127.0.0.1.
  • Nothing leaves without youJira and your sheet get the worklogs you send. Claude sees your work only if you connect it, and looking for a new version asks for a number, nothing more.
  • Private stays privateIncognito tabs are never recorded; apps and sites you exclude are kept only as "private".
  • Gone when you say soOld activity is deleted after 90 days, and any hour of a day can be forgotten at once.
